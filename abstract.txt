Frameworks for multi-sample long-read RNA-seq analysis

Michael Love, Beatriz Campillo, Justin Landis
UNC-Chapel Hill, Genetics, Chapel Hill, NC, United States

Long-read RNA sequencing allows capture of near-complete transcript structure,
offering far more information per molecule than short reads. Multi-sample
methods designed for short-read data do not immediately transfer directly to
this setting, creating an opportunity for new methods that exploit this richer
signal. We present open-source frameworks built on R/Bioconductor, the tidyomics
project, and the oarfish quantification tool that address current gaps in
provenance detection, event detection, and isoform-level inference for
multi-sample long-read RNA-seq. Provenance-aware quantification has previously
been ensured by tools such as tximeta, which recognizes a digest of the
reference transcript sequences to link quantifications to their
provenance. Long-read workflows often quantify against a mixed set of annotated
reference transcripts and novel transcripts, so we extend strategies to cover
reference-plus-novel annotations rather than a fixed reference alone. For event
detection, short-read methods often inferred splicing events by counting reads
spanning exon-exon junctions at non-constitutive exons, for example, or similar
strategies for other events. Sequencing nearly the entire transcript yields more
complete observations, allowing us to connect events at the transcript level and
replace spanning-read ratio counting with statistical modeling of read counts
that includes bias modeling. This still requires dedicated software to identify
splicing events, coupled to quantitation and differential models. Long-read data
also yield a wealth of isoform-level information, but meaningful biological
differences may reside in sub-gene structure that is blurred or distributed
across many expressed but similar isoforms. Fragmentation and other biases can
further produce spurious expression across similar isoforms. We develop and
evaluate novel methods for aggregation of isoforms informed by differential
signal which still maintain control of false discovery rate. This work is
available through three open source tools: tximeta (provenance detection),
splicelogic (event detection), and plyxp (flexible dataset manipulation and
aggregation). Together these tools lower the barrier to rigorous multi-sample
long-read analysis.
